Spacing math

These numbers assume the default Orbit Studio settings: about 300 extracted frames and 18 views per frame.

1.0 m

Ideal standoff from walls and key objects. Texture needs room to be seen.

0.3 m

Absolute minimum distance. Closer than this and the stitch and the solve both degrade.

0.5 m/s

Walking pace ceiling. Slower than feels natural, smooth heel-to-toe steps.

0.25 m

Effective distance between extracted frames at that pace. Even spacing beats bursts.

60-80 %

View overlap between neighboring frames that COLMAP wants for confident matching.

1-3 min

Per room. Longer takes add blur risk and upload weight, not quality.

Multi-room protocol

  1. One continuous take for the whole floor. Splitting rooms into separate clips breaks the pose graph between them.
  2. Lock exposure and clean both lenses before you start. Standard color, sharpness low, AdaptiveTone off, around 1/500 shutter indoors.
  3. Order rooms so the hallway is your spine. Enter each room from the hall, finish it fully, return to the hall. Never shortcut through an unscanned space.
  4. Perimeter loop first, then an interior sweep. The loop anchors the room, the sweep fills the middle with parallax.
  5. Cross every doorway straight and slow. Doorways are where reconstructions tear. Hold the camera steady, no turning inside the threshold.
  6. Close every loop. Re-see the start of each room before leaving, and end the take where it began. Loop closure is what kills drift.
  7. Two heights in hero rooms. Repeat the loop at chest height where detail matters most.
  8. Keep the scene frozen. Lights on, blinds set, pets and people out, doors either fully open or fully closed for the whole take.

Why this works

Overlap feeds the solver

COLMAP registers a frame only when it shares enough visual features with its neighbors. Even spacing at 60 to 80 percent overlap keeps every frame connected to the graph.

Standoff preserves texture

Inside 0.3 m, walls become featureless blurs and the dual-lens stitch distorts. At about 1 m, surfaces carry the texture the matcher needs.

Loops cancel drift

Every step adds a little pose error. Returning to a place the solver has already seen forces the accumulated error back to near zero.
