Bridge Capture Paths, 1 to 3 Spans

One walk that serves two masters: every required NBIS photo stop lands on the path, and the path itself is engineered so COLMAP recovers precise poses and the splat holds together over and under the deck. Camera rides the pole overhead, hands stay free for the inspection.

The one problem that decides everything

Deck top and deck underside share almost no visual features. Captured separately, they reconstruct as two disconnected islands and the model is worthless for measurement. The fix is geometric: descend the embankment at the corners while recording, so approach, wing wall, abutment face, fascia, and soffit all appear in the same frames. Tie at least two diagonal corners, all four for precision work.

Single span

1 span, no piers

2 support lines, 2 joint sightings per curb, 2 bearing lines, 4 corner ties, about 20 to 30 minutes
Why this is enough for one span: the deck loop and the underside lattice each close on themselves, four green corner ties weld the two together, and the two-distance elevation passes give the solver long-baseline frames that pin global scale and straightness.

Two spans

2 spans, 1 pier

3 support lines, 3 joint sightings per curb, 3 bearing lines, 1 pier orbit, about 35 to 50 minutes
What changes with the pier: the underside becomes two lattices joined through a full orbit of the pier. Enter the orbit from span 1, circle it completely seeing both faces plus every bearing on the cap, exit into span 2. The orbit is the strongest node in the whole pose graph, so give it the slowest, smoothest 90 seconds of the take. Sight the third expansion joint on the deck directly above it.

Three spans

3 spans, 2 piers

4 support lines, 4 joint sightings per curb, 4 bearing lines, 2 pier orbits, mid-length re-anchors, about 50 to 75 minutes
What changes at three spans: drift along the length becomes the enemy. Two pier orbits act as hubs, the serpentine alternates direction span to span so you exit at the far abutment without backtracking, and the elevation passes gain deliberate mid-span pause stations at both distances. Those long perpendicular frames re-anchor the chain the way loop closure does indoors. On bridges past about 250 ft, add a fifth far pass at 20 m if the site allows.

Phase order, every bridge

1Deck loopApproach A, down one curb, approach B, back the other curb. Pause 2 seconds sighting along every expansion joint. Barriers and wearing surface land automatically.
2Corner tie downDescend the embankment at the first corner, slow and continuous. This weld frame set is the most valuable 15 seconds of the take.
3Elevation passes, near then farWalk parallel to the fascia at about 3 m, again at 8 to 12 m, both sides. Cover wings and abutment faces square-on at each end.
4Bearing line passLateral pass along each support line at knee to chest height, every beam seat, sole plate to pad to masonry plate in frame.
5Underside latticeSerpentine the bays span by span, pole up for soffit and cross-frames, mid-bay and support ends per NBIS. Route through each pier orbit as the hub between spans.
6Channel arcsUpstream and downstream arcs with both faces and both banks in frame, connected along one bank so the arcs join the graph.
7Close the loopTie up the far corner, re-walk a short deck segment, end within sight of your start point. The solver zeroes accumulated drift against it.

Precision rules on structure

1-1.5 m

Standoff from girders, soffit, and pier faces. Never inside 0.5 m of steel, the stitch and the matcher both suffer.

2 distances

Every large planar face gets a near detail pass and a far geometry pass. Near frames carry defects, far frames carry shape.

0.3 m

Frame spacing on structure at slow walk. Half speed again inside bearing lines and orbits.

2 s

Dwell at every expansion joint, sighting along it. Doubles as the NBIS joint photo and a parallax anchor at the support line.

2+ targets

Known-dimension scale references at opposite abutment corners. Splats have no native scale, targets make the model measurable.

1 take

Exposure locked before the first step, one continuous recording. If you must split, overlap 20 seconds of path at the seam.
