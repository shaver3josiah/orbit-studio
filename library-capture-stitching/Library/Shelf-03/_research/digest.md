### SLICE: Practitioner/community workflows (2026) for turning Insta360 photos/video into Gaussian splats: capture, reframe, and reconstruction recipes for small (~10-30) panorama counts
[SUMMARY] Two distinct community tracks exist in 2026. Track two is the DIY/local pipeline closer to Orbit Studio's existing design: equirectangular frames are reprojected into multiple perspective/cubemap views (6 cubemap faces with ~10% overlap is the most-cited split, per Mikhail Mokrushin's workflow writeup) because COLMAP historically could not consume spherical images directly -- Jonathan Stephens' tutorial calls horizontal-slicing "the EASIEST but not the BEST way" for exactly this reason. That constraint changed: COLMAP's panorama_sfm path added native spherical/equirectangular camera models plus a much faster GPU bundle-adjustment backend (Caspar), removing the crop/re-stitch step entirely for practitioners willing to use the CLI (not yet exposed in the GUI, confirmed via COLMAP issue #3595) -- though the exact release version/date could not be pinned down with confidence (sources conflict on 2024 vs 2026). Nadir/operator handling has shifted from manual "nadir patch" compositing toward automated masking: a solo developer's "3DGaussianTools" (realitystack Substack, May 2026) reprojects each pano to 18 virtual cameras, runs YOLO+SAM masking, and dilates masks ~15% to remove tripod/selfie-stick/shadow artifacts before training in Nerfstudio or LichtFeld Studio. A cluster of small paid Windows utilities (360 Splat Pro, 360 Video Stills Prep Tool, Metashape-to-COLMAP converter) and one open-source single-panorama tool (SPAG4d, MIT core) round out the tooling, but none specifically report results on ~10-30 discrete panorama stills of a static structure like a bridge -- that remains the least-covered scenario in current community writeups.

### SLICE: Sparse-view / few-shot Gaussian splatting (~10-30 camera stations): InstantSplat lineage, depth-prior regularization, 2025-2026 few-shot 3DGS papers, and the 360-photo-to-cubemap angle
[SUMMARY] Sparse-view 3DGS has moved fast over the last year along two axes relevant to a 20-station bridge shoot. First, pose/init: classical COLMAP struggles when only ~20 widely-spaced stations exist, so the field has shifted to feed-forward pointmap networks (DUSt3R -> MASt3R -> VGGT -> pi3) that jointly predict poses+depth+points and explicitly target "sparse and low-overlap" capture — exactly this use case; VGGT's code became commercial-use-friendly (excluding military) on 2025-07-29. Second, regularization: since InstantSplat (2024) established DUSt3R-init plus fast optimization for sparse views, a wave of 2025-2026 papers (FSGS, DNGaussian, CoR-GS, SparseGS, MS-GS, Pi-GS, DropAnSH-GS, SaveWildGS) converged on a shared recipe — monocular-depth-prior losses (Pearson correlation, not raw L2), pseudo/virtual-view interpolation between real cameras, and anchor-cluster or spherical-harmonic-coefficient dropout — to fight the two dominant failure modes: near-camera floaters and view-overfitting, rather than relying on plain opacity-reset. Pi-GS (Feb 2026) beats InstantSplat/FSGS/DNGaussian on Tanks&Temples 3-view and also reports MipNeRF360 12/24-view outdoor results. A separate but directly relevant line handles 360/panoramic captures natively: ODGS and Splatter-360 do spherical-domain (cubemap or ERP) rasterization instead of independent perspective crops, and PanoImager/LighthouseGS explicitly flag that sparse panoramic stations degrade cross-view consistency and extrapolation. No paper tests exactly "20 outdoor bridge panoramas," but MS-GS and SaveWildGS (in-the-wild, distractors, appearance variation across sessions) and Seam360GS (dual-fisheye seam/distortion calibration) each match a specific real-world hazard this shoot will hit: traffic/pedestrian ghosting, lighting drift between stations, and stitching seams. A practical (non-peer-reviewed) blog independently converged on splitting each 360 frame into 18 perspective views (a ring of 16 plus 2 poles) rather than a plain 6-face cubemap, precisely to answer the "many perspective views per panorama" question.

### SLICE: Diffusion/video-prior artifact repair and view densification for sparse-view 3DGS (Difix3D+, GSFixer, GSFix3D, VidSplat, OracleGS, GaMO)

Maturity and licensing vary sharply. NVIDIA Difix3D+ (CVPR 2025 Oral) is the most usable today: public HuggingFace weights (nvidia/difix, nvidia/difix_ref), a single-step SD-Turbo-scale model (cheap versus the rest), and a documented nerfstudio/gsplat post-processing hook -- but under NVIDIA's non-commercial research license plus a Stability AI sub-license. ### SLICE: Panorama-native pose-free reconstruction: PanoSplatt3R (ICCV 2025) and the OmniSplat/PanSplat family
[SUMMARY] 
PanoSplatt3R (github.com/zhichu99/PanoSplatt3R, arXiv 2507.21960, ICCV 2025) is fully code-and-weights released: its TODO checklist (Evaluation Code, Training Code, Model Checkpoint, Test Dataset) is entirely checked, with weights hosted on HuggingFace (zhichu99/PanoSplatt3R). However, the GitHub API reports license:null and no LICENSE file exists in the repo -- it is publicly visible but not explicitly open-sourced. It is strictly a 2-panorama (pairwise), pose-free model built on a MASt3R/DUSt3R-style ViT backbone with a novel "RoPE rolling" position encoding for horizontal panorama periodicity; it ships no >2-view global-alignment tool. Its only public training/test datasets are HM3D and Replica (both indoor); no outdoor demo or benchmark is shown despite the paper's abstract claiming indoor/outdoor generalization. Training needs >=48GB VRAM (NVIDIA L40 used); inference-specific VRAM is undocumented anywhere in the repo, paper, or project page. The repo has been dormant since Oct 17, 2025 (last push), with open unresolved issues about custom/unposed inference.

OmniSplat (github.com/esw0116/OmniSplat, arXiv 2412.16604, CVPR 2025 Highlight) is MIT-licensed and, notably, is the only one of the three with genuine outdoor training/eval coverage (360Roam, OmniPhotos, OmniBlender/Ricoh360, OmniScenes, 360VO). But it is not pose-free -- it runs on posed MVSplat and requires OpenMVG-derived SfM poses per scene -- and carries a known, unpatched equirectangular pixel-misalignment bug plus an unanswered "how do I use my own data" issue. Dormant since June 6, 2025.

PanSplat (github.com/chengzhag/PanSplat, arXiv 2412.12096, CVPR 2025) is MIT-licensed, actively maintained into 2026 (commits through Apr 19, 2026, though recent ones are minor/cosmetic), and offers downloadable checkpoints up to 4K (2048x4096) -- but the 4K model needs >=24GB VRAM (RTX 3090-class, trained on A100), ruling out a free T4. It is also not pose-free (real-video pipeline relies on stella_vslam SLAM poses). Training data is indoor-dominant (Matterport3D/Replica/Residential) with 360Loc real-world fine-tuning data that does include outdoor urban/park scenes.

As a baseline comparison, facebookresearch/map-anything (Apache-2.0 code) is far more active in this window (four 2026 releases through Jul 17, 2026) and now offers a fully Apache-2.0 checkpoint tier (map-anything-apache-v1) alongside its default CC-BY-NC-4.0 weights -- but it has no native panorama/equirectangular input, so the perspective-split step Orbit Studio already uses remains necessary.
