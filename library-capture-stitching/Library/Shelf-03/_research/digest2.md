### SLICE: Papers/code that train 3D Gaussian Splatting directly from equirectangular/360 panoramas (no perspective-crop preprocessing) — OmniGS, 360-GS, ODGS, Splatter-360, PanSplat, OmniSplat, ErpGS, PanoGS, TPGS, and newer 2026 work
[SUMMARY] Direct-from-equirect 3DGS is an active but still academic-maturity field as of July 2026. Real, runnable, per-scene optimization code exists for OmniGS (WACV 2025, GPL-3.0, C++/CUDA equirect rasterizer) and ODGS (NeurIPS 2024, MIT, PyTorch) — both require poses from OpenMVG, not COLMAP, since COLMAP has no spherical camera model. 360-GS (Feb 2024) also has public code (LeoDarcy/360GS) using tangent-plane projection with a custom panorama rasterizer, indoor-focused. The self-calibrating/pose-free option that would most help a sparse-still capture, SC-OmniGS (ICLR 2025), has only a project webpage on GitHub, not training code. Newer 2026 work (Spherical-GOF, CylinderSplat, UniTriSplat, PFGS360, ODGS-SLAM) pushes geometry quality and pose-freeness further but is either code-not-yet-released, video-sequence-oriented (not discrete stills), or unverified in this research pass. ErpGS and TPGS address outdoor 360-rig distortion and single-panorama reconstruction respectively, but ErpGS's code is "coming soon" and TPGS's repo is incomplete/undocumented. Feed-forward generalizable methods (Splatter-360, PanSplat, OmniSplat) are the most code-complete and well-maintained (pretrained checkpoints, active repos), but solve 2-view interpolation for indoor synthetic benchmarks, not many-view outdoor scene fusion. SPaGS (EGSR/CGF 2025) is notable for an explicit outdoor drone-panorama dataset but ships as a plugin to a heavyweight research framework. Bottom line: perspective-split remains the practical route for Orbit Studio today, primarily because it lets you keep COLMAP for pose recovery; direct-equirect training is promising but not a drop-in replacement.
  * REC: Do not swap pycolmap out yet: none of the direct-equirect papers use COLMAP for pose recovery (it has no spherical/equirectangular camera model) — they use OpenMVG (OmniGS, ODGS) or OpenSfM (the hobby recipe below). Adopting direct-equirect training means also replacing the SfM stage, which is a bigger, riskier pipeline change than it first appears for only 20 outdoor stills.
  * REC: If you want to prototype direct-from-equirect training, start from esw0116/ODGS (MIT, NeurIPS 2024) rather than OmniGS (GPL-3.0, heavier CUDA/LibTorch/OpenCV-CUDA native build) — ODGS is Python/PyTorch-ish, permissively licensed, and closer to your existing gsplat/Brush stack, but expect a stale repo (no commits since Dec 2024) and the need to wire in OpenMVG for poses yourself.
  * REC: Treat SC-OmniGS, Spherical-GOF, ErpGS, and TPGS as watch-only for now: each claims strong outdoor or pose-free results but has no runnable public training code as of July 2026 (SC-OmniGS repo is a project webpage only; Spherical-GOF repo has just README+LICENSE with code 'will be released'; ErpGS project page literally says 'Code (Coming Soon)'; TPGS repo has 17 commits but an incomplete README with no working setup instructions).
  * REC: Skip the feed-forward pair methods (PanSplat, Splatter-360, OmniSplat) for this goal — they solve 2-view interpolation/generalization, not fusing ~20 wide-baseline stills into one consistent walkable scene, and are validated mainly on indoor synthetic benchmarks (Matterport3D, HM3D, Replica). PanSplat's Insta360-video fine-tune is the most real-world-relevant checkpoint of the three but is still pairwise, not a full-scene reconstruction from N stills.
  * REC: Best low-risk experiment: keep your current pycolmap perspective-crop SfM exactly as-is (it is proven on outdoor unbounded scenes), but aggregate the recovered per-crop poses back into one pose per source equirect frame and feed that into an ODGS/OmniGS-style equirect rasterizer for the final gsplat training pass instead of the resampled perspective crops. This captures the no-crop-seam benefit of direct-equirect training without abandoning the SfM stage that already works for you.
  * REC: Warn the user up front that 20 stills is sparse for a large outdoor scene (a bridge plus surrounding roadways): every panorama-GS paper surveyed here demonstrates on dozens-to-hundreds of frames or continuous video, not ~20 discrete stills. Recommend either capturing more panoramas spaced along the roadway/under the deck, or leaning on a monocular-depth prior (as LighthouseGS, ErpGS, and the hobbyist SPAG4d tool do) to regularize the sparse regions, regardless of which splat variant is ultimately used.
  * REC: For a quick practical spike (not a citation-grade research contribution), inuex35/360-gaussian-splatting is the closest existing recipe to 'N equirect stills -> OpenSfM spherical poses -> panorama-aware 3DGS training' end to end, and worth cloning to see how far it gets on a bridge dataset before investing in any of the CVPR/ICLR papers above — but it is an unpublished hobby repo with unverified maintenance state, so budget time for debugging.

### SLICE: Outdoor infrastructure splat quality: sky handling, appearance/exposure correction, anti-aliasing, floater pruning, transient-object masking, nadir/tripod holes, scale calibration/georeferencing
[SUMMARY] Since roughly late 2025, open-source 3DGS tooling has converged on a fairly standard "outdoor quality checklist" that is now mostly flag-toggles rather than custom code. gsplat's simple_trainer.py exposes app_opt (per-image appearance embedding), post_processing={bilateral_grid,ppisp} for exposure/white-balance drift, and an antialiased flag implementing Mip-Splatting's 2D/3D filter (though gsplat issue #840 shows it can delete thin high-frequency detail). StopThePop's per-ray depth sorting remains a standalone fork (r4dl/StopThePop), not merged into gsplat/nerfstudio mainline. Sky handling has moved from "just let it floater" to explicit sky masks (skyseg U-2-Net/onnx) or dedicated sky Gaussians/skyboxes (spirulae-splat's background_mode=sh, PostShot's sky sphere, the March-2026 R3GW paper). Transient-object handling for moving cars/pedestrians now has both automatic methods (SpotLessSplats' diffusion-feature+residual masking, RobustSplat's delayed-densification + scale-cascaded mask bootstrapping, ICCV 2025) and practical mask-generation tooling (YOLO/SAM2.1/SAM3.1 in stechdrive-3dgs-utils, MIT, v1.25.2 July 2026). Floater pruning has both research methods (TIDI-GS, Clean-GS, both Jan 2026 arXiv) and the de facto manual tool, SuperSplat (MIT, browser-based lasso/crop cleanup). For 360-specific capture, COLMAP 4.1.0 (June 26, 2026) added a native EQUIRECTANGULAR spherical camera model (faster but admittedly less accurate than perspective rendering per COLMAP's own notes), while SPAG-4D and stechdrive-3dgs-utils both use cubemap/pole-cap face-splitting to handle nadir/tripod holes and offer AprilTag-based metric scale calibration as an alternative to GPS EXIF, which Insta360's JPG equirect exports typically lack.
  * REC: Replace the ffmpeg-frame-extraction step (irrelevant for 20 stills) with a cubemap/perspective-face split per panorama before COLMAP, rather than relying on COLMAP 4.1's new native EQUIRECTANGULAR camera model alone -- COLMAP's own 4.1.0 release notes call native spherical reconstruction 'generally faster but less accurate than rendering perspective views,' and with only 20 source stations pose accuracy matters more than speed.
  * REC: Add a per-face masking pre-pass before COLMAP feature extraction: sky mask (skyseg U-2-Net/onnx, MIT) + tripod/nadir mask, fed to COLMAP via ImageReader.mask_path so no SfM features are matched on sky or the tripod blob -- directly reduces the floater count gsplat/Brush will otherwise have to prune later.
  * REC: If any of the 20 panoramas show moving cars or pedestrians on the roadway, run YOLO/SAM2.1 (or SAM3.1 prompt masks) to produce ignore-region masks and pass them into gsplat's dataset `mask` field (or spirulae-splat's `apply_loss_for_mask`) during training, not just at the COLMAP stage -- this is the same approach stechdrive-3dgs-utils and RobustSplat/SpotLessSplats use to stop transient objects from becoming ghost splats.
  * REC: Turn on gsplat's existing appearance-correction flags for training: `app_opt=True` (16-dim per-image embedding) and `post_processing='bilateral_grid'` or `'ppisp'` (spirulae-splat reports PPISP gives less color shift) -- this is a config change, not new code, and directly targets exposure/white-balance drift across 20 shots taken at different times of day.
  * REC: A/B test gsplat's `antialiased` (Mip-Splatting) flag on the bridge scene specifically -- gsplat issue #840 documents it deleting thin high-frequency structures (bike spokes, by analogy cables/railings), which matters for a bridge's guardrails and cable geometry; don't assume anti-aliasing is a free win here.
  * REC: Add an explicit scale-calibration step since 20 tripod-mounted 360 stills won't carry a GPS track the way drone footage does (Insta360 JPG equirect export typically drops GPS unless a GPX/embed option is explicitly used): either place one printed AprilTag of known size in a couple of frames (as stechdrive-3dgs-utils does) or feed COLMAP's model_aligner one or two known real-world distances (railing post spacing, lane width) as control points -- otherwise the splat is unscaled and can't be measured or georeferenced.
  * REC: Add a cheap manual cleanup pass with SuperSplat (browser-based, MIT, playcanvas/supersplat) before shipping artifact.splat: crop scene bounds and lasso-remove stray sky/water floaters -- no new dependency, it's already the community-standard post-processing step.
  * REC: For the nadir tripod hole in each of the 20 stations, don't attempt generative inpainting (overkill for 20 stills) -- either mask-and-accept the hole (simplest) or crop/plane-fill it in SuperSplat afterward; reserve pole-cap-with-seam-latitude techniques (SPAG-4D's approach) only if a filled floor patch under each station is actually needed for the fly-through.
