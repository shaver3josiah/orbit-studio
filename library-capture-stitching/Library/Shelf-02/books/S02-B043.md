# Common Failure Signatures: Texture-Poor Rooms in the Sparse Model

How do you tell, just by looking at the sparse point cloud, that a room failed because it was texture-poor, and not for some other reason? This matters because the fix is different for every failure class. A room that failed from motion blur needs a reshoot with a faster shutter. A room that failed from a broken capture path needs more overlap. A texture-poor room, an empty hallway, a white conference room, a hotel room with blank walls and matching linens, needs neither. It needs a different signature to be recognized in the first place, or the operator ends up reshooting the same bare walls the same way and getting the same thin result.

The sibling volume Why Some Rooms Register and Others Don't lays out the general question of registration success. This book is narrower: it catalogs what texture-poor rooms specifically look like once they've already gone through COLMAP, so you can read a sparse model and know which failure you're looking at without re-deriving it from theory each time.

## The signature: thin clusters, not absence

A texture-poor room does not fail to produce points. It produces too few of them, and they cluster in the wrong places. Open points3D.txt (documented in COLMAP's Output Format reference) and you'll see the pattern directly: a healthy room gives you a dense, evenly distributed scatter across every visible surface. A texture-poor room gives you point clusters concentrated at the few features that do carry texture, edges of furniture, a light switch, the seam where two wall colors meet, and almost nothing across the wide blank stretches between them. The blank drywall, the plain ceiling, the matching bedspread all register as gaps.

This is a direct consequence of how feature extraction works. COLMAP's default feature_extractor pulls up to 8192 features per image using SIFT (with RootSIFT normalization by default), and SIFT finds features by looking for distinctive local gradient patterns. A blank wall has almost no local gradient variation to find. Fewer detected features means fewer candidates for matching, means fewer triangulated points, means a visibly sparser cluster in exactly the regions where the wall was blank. You can see this bias in the raw output before you even get to registration statistics: count how many of the total per-image points fall in the featureless regions of the room versus the textured accents, and the imbalance itself is the signature.

## Track length: short and broken, not just few

The second dimension is track length, the TRACK[] list in points3D.txt (IMAGE_ID, POINT2D_IDX pairs) that COLMAP logs for every triangulated point. A well-textured room's points get seen and matched across many images, long tracks. A texture-poor room's few points tend to have short, broken tracks, because a feature that's marginal to begin with is also more likely to fail to match consistently frame to frame, particularly as illumination shifts slightly across the room. COLMAP's FAQ notes this directly: weak-texture scenes are exactly the case where the documentation recommends DSP-SIFT with affine feature-shape estimation, guided feature matching, and disabling ignore_two_view_tracks so two-view-only tracks still get triangulated instead of thrown away. That recommendation exists because in a texture-poor room, a lot of what little structure you get is going to arrive as two-view tracks, and discarding them by default (as ignore_two_view_tracks would) throws away most of what the room had to offer. The FAQ's own framing is worth keeping close: these problems appear "particularly for scenes with limited view overlap, little scene texture, and drastic illumination changes."

The console log gives you the same signal in real time. COLMAP's incremental mapper prints lines like "Registering image #241 (230)" followed by "=> Image sees 3755 / 8696 points" for each image as it registers. In a texture-poor room, watch that ratio: images from inside the room show a much lower points-seen count relative to the totals than images from adjacent, better-textured rooms in the same walkthrough. That's a live, per-image readout of the same thin-cluster problem you'd otherwise only see after the fact in points3D.txt.

## Distinguishing it from a broken track (the other failure mode)

It's worth being precise about what texture-poverty is not, because the other common signature it gets confused with is a broken registration path, where the capture sequence itself has a gap (a door swung shut between two rooms, a jump the operator took that broke sequential overlap) and COLMAP ends up reconstructing multiple disconnected models instead of one connected one, exactly as the Tutorial docs describe when not all images end up registered together. A broken path shows up as a hard split: two clean, well-populated point clouds that just don't share a rigid transform. Texture poverty shows up differently: one continuous model, but with a visibly starved density in the affected room compared to its neighbors. The tell is density and track length within a connected model, not connectivity itself. If you're triaging a sparse model and you see one full split, that's the broken-path failure. If you see one connected model with an anemic patch, that's texture poverty. Automating a Reshoot Request from a Failed Registration depends on making exactly this distinction correctly, because the corrective instruction to the operator is different in each case: reshoot with denser overlap and a manual initial pair for a broken path, versus reshoot with denser overlap and better light or more surface variation for a texture-poor room.

## What actually helps at the sparse-model stage

The notes support a short, specific list of mitigations at the SfM stage itself, independent of what happens at the capture stage (which the sibling volume Texture-Poor Walls and Why Blank Surfaces Fail to Register covers from the physical-capture side). COLMAP's FAQ recommends, for weak-texture scenes: DSP-SIFT with affine feature-shape estimation, guided feature matching, and disabling ignore_two_view_tracks. Separately, the FAQ notes that ALIKED features can produce more repeatable matches than SIFT specifically in scenes with limited overlap, little texture, and drastic illumination change, which is worth trying as a straight feature-extractor swap when SIFT is visibly starving in a given room. None of these turn a blank wall into a textured one, but they recover more of the marginal signal that is there rather than discarding it by default.

## What it means for the reader

If you're the operator or the engineer building the reshoot logic, the practical upshot is this: don't wait for a hard registration failure to flag a texture-poor room. Read the sparse model itself. A room with a visibly thinner point cluster and shorter, patchier tracks than its neighbors, inside an otherwise connected model, is texture poverty, and it's diagnosable directly from points3D.txt density and the per-image "sees N / M points" ratio in the mapper log, without needing the room to fail outright. Recognizing that signature early, and recognizing it as distinct from a broken capture path, is what lets an automated reshoot request send the operator back with the right instruction instead of a generic "try again."
